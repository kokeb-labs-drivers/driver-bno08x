Transports¶

One class, four ways to reach the hub. The port you write names its transport; everything above it (operations, events, calibration) is identical. The strap pins PS1 and PS0 choose the transport in the chip, so the port's kTransport must match the wiring.

SHTP in one page¶

Every message is a cargo with a 4-byte header: a 15-bit length (the top bit continues a cargo), a channel and a per-channel sequence number. The driver uses channel 0 (the advertisement and the error list), 1 (executable commands), 2 (SH-2 control), 3 and 4 (input reports, normal and wake-up) and 5 (the gyro-integrated rotation vector). Cargoes can be fragmented by the hub; the driver reassembles them and never fragments what it sends: a request that does not fit one write is refused with Oversize. The advertisement at start-up carries the limits; without a limit tag the driver uses its Limits sizes (A12).

The framing engine is strict. Every rule it enforces has a counter in Stats (short transfers, a length of 0xFFFF, a null header, a length of 1 to 3, an oversize cargo, a bad channel, lost or orphaned continuations, wrong continuation lengths, duplicate or skipped sequence numbers, unknown or cut-short reports). Config::resync_limit violations within Config::resync_window_ms make the driver abandon the running operations with Framing, count a resynchronisation and reset the hub, instead of guessing at the bytes.

I2C¶

The hub is a slave at 0x4A (SA0 low) or 0x4B (SA0 high). The driver reads only after H_INTN went low: first the 4-byte header, then exactly the rest of the cargo as a continuation, each as a separate transaction that ends with a STOP (no repeated start). Reading without H_INTN is possible with Config::poll_without_int, which is guarded: a BNO085 stretches the clock when it has nothing to say, a BNO086 answers with a zero-length packet (A2), so the port documents its stretch timeout. Keep the bus at 400 kbit/s or less (A39).

SPI¶

Mode 3 (clock idles high, data sampled on the second edge), most significant bit first, at most 3 MHz, full duplex. H_INTN tells the host a cargo is waiting; the host then does one duplex transfer, which may be longer than the cargo (the hub pads with zeros and a null header). To write, the driver drives WAKE low if H_INTN is not already asserted, waits for H_INTN up to Config::wake_timeout_us, does the transfer and releases WAKE. PS0 is both a strap and the WAKE line, so it must be a GPIO that is high while NRST is released: the driver drives it high before every NRST pulse and releases it on every exit (operation time-out, HardReset(), resync, hub restart), DS p18.

UART with SHTP¶

Frames start and end with 0x7E; inside a frame 0x7E and 0x7D are escaped (0x7D, then the byte XOR 0x20). The byte after the start flag is the protocol ID: 1 for SHTP data, 0 for the hub's control frames (the buffer status query, BSQ, and the buffer status notification, BSN, in which the hub says how many bytes it can take). The BSN is a token: it holds until the next host write or until the advertised UART timeout (tag 0x81) expires, and the driver sends only while it is valid and large enough. The SHTP gives no answer time for a BSQ, so a query whose BSN has not arrived after Config::uart_bsq_retry_ms is sent again (Stats::bsq_retries) until the operation's own limit ends it; a lost query or notification therefore costs one retry, never the link. A write that times out half-sent is closed with a flag byte and never finished later, and a reset or resync drops a frame in progress. The hub needs at least 100 microseconds between bytes it receives, so the port either sends byte by byte through UartWriteByte (the driver paces it) or declares kPortPacesUartTx = true and implements UartWrite. Both UART modes need the 32.768 kHz clock on the board: the internal oscillator is not accurate enough at these rates (A10).

UART-RVC¶

The hub streams a 19-byte frame at 100 Hz and takes no commands: 0xAA 0xAA, an index that increases by one, yaw, pitch and roll in 0.01 degree, acceleration in milli-g, the BNO086's MI and MR bytes, reserved bytes and a checksum (the low byte of the sum of bytes 2 to 17, A6). Poll() delivers each valid frame as an EventKind::Rvc event whose v holds index, yaw, pitch, roll, ax, ay, az, mi, mr, mi_mr_valid. An ASCII banner at power-up is skipped (A9); a frame with a wrong checksum is counted and the scanner slides one byte. The $MIB text inputs the manual describes (reset, intent, dynamic calibration) are refused with Unsupported unless rvc_inputs_experimental is set, because the manual's own example checksum does not match its text (A8).
